# Demo Enclosure — Water Leakage Overpressure Transient
## Technical Summary for Presentation

---

## 1. What Is Being Modeled

A large industrial furnace/reactor enclosure (14,700 ft³ / ~416 m³) filled with **Argon atmosphere** houses a **powerstick** (solar cell test article, water-cooled) inside a tall narrow **hot zone** (graphite-lined rectangular prism, 67.75" tall × 10.875" wide) surrounded by graphite heating blocks at temperatures between 1000–2400 °C.

**The accident scenario:** A water leak from the powerstick's cooling circuit introduces cold water (40 °C, subcooled) onto the hot graphite surfaces. The water undergoes **film boiling**, generating steam — and potentially H2 and CO via the water-gas reaction (C + H2O → CO + H2) at high graphite temperatures. The generated gas pressurizes the Argon-filled enclosure.

**The safety question:** Does the pressure exceed the enclosure's rated **1 inH2O (249 Pa) gauge pressure limit**? What structural loads does the pressure impose on the hot-zone walls, floor, and ceiling?

**Two scenarios studied:**

1. **Normal (powerstick inserted):** Water contacts the graphite liner inner wall, which is thermally shielded from the blocks. Vaporization rate is limited by liner thermal mass and radiation balance. Less severe.

2. **Liner break (catastrophic):** Water floods the entire hot-zone floor (18 m²) and contacts hot graphite directly. This is the bounding case and the primary focus of the two-zone analysis.

## 3. Model Structure

### Single-Zone Model (`simulate`)
- Hot zone treated as a single well-mixed gas volume
- Liner inner wall temperature solved from radiation + conduction balance
- Film boiling HTC from Berenson/Bromley correlation
- Outputs zone pressure vs time and peak structural loads

### Two-Zone Model (`simulate_two_zone`) — Primary Model
The enclosure is split into two coupled gas zones:

**Hot Zone**
- Isothermal at block temperature T_block
- Gas (steam + products) generated by film boiling on floor/walls
- Communicates with cold zone through a **1 ft × 1 ft floor orifice** (Cd = 0.61)
- Optional extra 1.25" circular wall orifices and edge seam gaps

**Cold Zone**
- Remainder of enclosure (~416 m³ minus hot zone)
- Split into two sub-zones for thermal stratification:
  - *Mixed sub-zone* (upper 50%, near orifice/exhaust): heats rapidly from inflow
  - *Unmixed sub-zone* (lower 50%): relaxes toward mixed temperature with time constant τ ≈ 10 s
- Pressure computed using harmonic-weighted average temperature (thermodynamically exact)
- Gas exhausts through stack + optional emergency duct

**Exhaust Model**
- Buoyancy-driven flow up the stack using full friction model (Darcy-Weisbach with entry/exit losses)
- Exhaust louver: opens at 0.30 inH2O gauge, closes at 0.28 inH2O
- Louver itself adds 0.15 inH2O pressure drop
- Emergency duct runs in parallel (K_entry = 0.5, K_exit = 0.2)

**Optional features:**
- 400 kW chiller (PID-controlled, removes heat from cold zone)
- Argon backfill purge (volumetric flow rate in CFM)
- Semi-infinite FD floor transient (temperature-dependent graphite conductivity)
- Water-gas reaction (C + H2O → CO + H2, ΔH = +131 kJ/mol, thermodynamic equilibrium fraction vs temperature)

## 4. Key Physics

### Film Boiling Heat Transfer
- **Berenson/Bromley correlation** for pool film boiling
- **Rohsenow correlation** for nucleate boiling (Csf = 0.013)
- Transition zone (nucleate → film) blended over configurable ramp time
- Leidenfrost point: T_sat + 200 K ≈ 300 °C above saturation
- Steam superheat handled by Shomate Cp polynomial above 1000 °C (CoolProp IAPWS-95 below)
- Effective enthalpy rise: latent heat + subcooling sensible heat + superheat

### Water-Gas Reaction
- C + H2O → CO + H2, endothermic at +131.3 kJ/mol
- Equilibrium cracking fraction is temperature-dependent (from thermodynamic tables):
  - ~0% at 300 °C, ~44% at 600 °C, ~94% at 900 °C, ~99.9% at 1200 °C
- At block temperatures > 1000 °C, essentially complete conversion to H2 + CO

### Orifice Flow (Inter-Zone)
- Bidirectional: hot → cold when P_hot > P_cold, cold → hot (backflow) otherwise
- Supplemented by edge seam gaps (rectangular Hagen-Poiseuille with entry/exit losses)
- Additional 1.25" circular wall orifices with Cd = 0.61

## 6. Key Outputs and Safety Metrics

**Pressure**
- Peak hot zone gauge pressure [inH2O] vs 1 inH2O limit
- Peak cold zone gauge pressure [inH2O]
- Peak differential pressure hot − cold [inH2O, Pa]

**Structural loads at peak ΔP**
- Side wall force [kN] (4 walls × 18 m² total)
- Floor force [kN] (18.0 m²)
- Ceiling force [kN]

**Thermal**
- Peak mixed sub-zone temperature [°C] (= exhaust gas temperature)
- Peak unmixed sub-zone temperature [°C]
- Peak cold zone average temperature (pressure-equivalent) [°C]
- Floor surface temperature transient [°C]

**Flow**
- Peak orifice flow rate [CFM, m³/s]
- Peak exhaust stack flow rate [CFM]
- Chiller power vs time [kW]

**Gas composition (cold zone)**
- Peak H2 mole fraction [%]
- Peak CO mole fraction [%]
- Peak H2O mole fraction [%]
- Cumulative gas exhausted by species

**Water**
- Boiling rate [g/s, GPM equivalent]
- Cumulative water evaporated [gal] vs 7.5-gal supply

**General findings from parametric sweeps:**
- Increasing block temperature increases vaporization rate and peak pressure (more severe at higher T)
- Water-gas reaction (H2 + CO generation) becomes dominant above ~900 °C block temperature; at 2400 °C, nearly all steam converts to H2 + CO
- Emergency duct (3 ft diameter) substantially reduces peak pressure vs 6" stack alone
- Chiller (400 kW) reduces peak cold zone temperature but has limited effect on peak hot zone pressure (pressure is dominated by gas generation rate vs exhaust capacity, not cold zone temperature)
- Seam gaps along hot-zone edges provide meaningful additional relief area; even 1/8" seams reduce peak pressure noticeably
- Two-zone mixing model gives higher peak cold zone temperatures than full-mix assumption (stratification traps heat in upper zone); lower peak hot zone pressure than full-mix (upper mixed zone heats faster, reducing orifice ΔP)
- Floor transient (semi-infinite FD model) reduces effective boiling HTC over time as the graphite surface cools; extends the duration of significant vaporization compared to isothermal wall assumption
